For most of the last decade, AML teams treated "AI" the way they treated crypto in 2017. Interesting, vaguely menacing, and definitely not something to bring up in front of an FFIEC examiner.

That has changed. In the last 18 months, examiners themselves have started asking whether teams are *using* AI-assisted triage. The reasons aren't mysterious. False-positive rates haven't moved meaningfully in years. Examiners look at aging queues and triage backlogs and ask the same question the boards do: why is everything so slow?

## The shift in posture

The 2025 FinCEN AML/CFT Program Effectiveness statement was the inflection point. It changed the burden of proof: it's no longer enough to say you reviewed every alert. You have to show your program is *effective*, and "effective" is measured against benchmarks like SAR conversion rates, alert aging distributions, and false positive rates.

Once examiner conversations turned numeric, AI stopped being a risk and started being an answer.

## Five questions to ask any AI-AML vendor

When CompSync sits down with prospective compliance teams, we encourage them to ask these five questions of *any* AI-AML vendor, including us:

1. **Can you trace any alert back to a rule, a signal, and a threshold?** If the answer involves the word "embedding" without the word "explanation," walk away. Examiners will not accept an alert you cannot defend.
2. **Where does the model fit in your audit trail?** Every prediction the model makes should land somewhere reviewable. If "the model said so" is its own line item in your case log, you're fine. If it's invisible, you're not.
3. **What's your false-positive measurement methodology?** "Lower false positives" is meaningless unless the vendor can show you their numerator and denominator. Ask for the formula.
4. **How do you shadow new rules?** No production rule should go live without a shadow period against historical data. If the vendor can't describe theirs in one sentence, they don't have one.
5. **What happens during an examiner walkthrough?** Ask the vendor to show you a sample examiner walkthrough using their tool. The good vendors have one ready. The rest are still building it.

## The honest answer

AI doesn't reduce alerts. It reduces the *cost* of working alerts. A 90-second triage that used to take ten minutes is still a triage. The regulatory burden hasn't moved. What's moved is your team's ability to keep up with it.

That's the lever, and that's the conversation worth having with your next vendor.
